# ESC1.7 (GEC 1.7) : The company conducts an assessment to identify actual and potential environmental impacts connected to its operations and value chain.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise procède à une évaluation afin d'identifier les impacts environnementaux réels et potentiels liés à ses activités internes et à sa chaîne de valeur*

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 931-945. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**ESC1** : The company knows its actual and potential impacts on the environment.

**ESC1.7** : The company conducts an assessment to identify actual and potential environmental impacts connected to its operations and value chain.

## Compliance criteria

### 1.7.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company’s assessment:

- a) covers the company’s operations and value chain
- b) identifies any actual or potential negative impacts of the company's operations and value chain that may contribute to the five drivers of nature change
- c) identifies any actual or potential negative impacts of the company's operations and value chain on animal welfare
- d) is based on internal and external research, and engagement with stakeholders
- e) prioritizes issues based on severity and likelihood.

*Traduction FR (à relire, compilation tierce) :* 1.7.1 L'évaluation de l'entreprise :
a) Couvre les activités directes et la chaîne de valeur de l'entreprise ;
b) Identifie tout impact négatif réel ou potentiel des activités directes et de la chaîne de valeur de l'entreprise susceptible de contribuer aux cinq facteurs de changement de la nature ;
c) Identifie tout impact négatif réel ou potentiel des activités directes et de la chaîne de valeur de l'entreprise sur le bien-être animal ;
d) Est fondée sur des recherches internes et externes, ainsi que sur l'engagement des parties prenantes ;
e) Hiérarchise les sujets en fonction de leur gravité et de leur probabilité.

### 1.7.2

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The assessment includes:

- a) a high level overview of the company's value chain indicating where actual or potential negative impacts on nature and animal welfare have been identified
- b) a list of prioritized value chain activities and company operations that contribute to negative impacts on nature
- c) a list of high impact commodities or products in the supply chain, and an explanation of how they were identified
- d) a list of prioritized material environmental impact areas.

*Traduction FR (à relire, compilation tierce) :* 1.7.2 L'évaluation comprend :
a) Une vue d'ensemble de la chaîne de valeur de l'entreprise indiquant les impacts négatifs réels ou potentiels sur la nature et le bien-être animal ;
b) Une liste des activités prioritaires de la chaîne de valeur et des activités directes de l'entreprise qui contribuent aux impacts négatifs sur la nature ;
c) Une liste des matières premières ou des produits à fort impact dans la chaîne d'approvisionnement, et une explication de la manière dont ils ont été identifiés ;
d) Une liste des enjeux d'impacts environnementaux matériels classée par ordre de priorité.

### 1.7.3

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The assessment was completed or updated in the last 36 months.

*Traduction FR (à relire, compilation tierce) :* 1.7.3 L'évaluation a été réalisée ou mise à jour au cours des 36 derniers mois.

### 1.7.4

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

If water, biodiversity, and waste were not deemed material, the company records a written explanation why. The written explanation:

- a) includes credible scientific evidence
- b) is approved by the highest governing body or executive team.

*Traduction FR (à relire, compilation tierce) :* 1.7.4 Si l'eau, la biodiversité et les déchets n'ont pas été jugés matériels, l'entreprise en donne une explication écrite. L'explication écrite :
a) Comprend des preuves scientifiques crédibles ;
b) Est approuvée par la plus haute instance ou organe de direction.

### 1.7.5

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company considers any “potential impacts” from the Risk Tool (FR3.1) in its assessment.

## Intent

To identify actual and potential environmental impacts linked to different parts of the company’s value chain, including its operations, supply chain, product usage, and product end-of-life.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[1.7.1] The assessment under this sub-requirement is connected to the impact or double materiality assessment [PSG2.3]. However, this assessment includes more specific details than a materiality assessment, in the form of impact areas, commodities, and certain operations.

The assessment conducted under this requirement is a demonstration of due diligence, and provides a good base to inform the strategies and actions the company develops under this Impact Topic.

[1.7.1] A company includes the environmental impacts linked to the usage of their products or services if:

- There’s a clear cause-and-effect link between the product or service and the environmental impact. For instance, a company that sells chainsaws directly to a logging company has a direct connection to deforestation. But a retailer selling chainsaws to individual customers doesn’t have the same direct link since it’s harder to predict or measure the impact.
- It is reasonable to expect the company to have sufficient knowledge about the environmental impacts linked to the use of their product or service and mitigate them. For example, a retailer selling chainsaws to the general public likely wouldn’t know how each chainsaw will be used or the environmental consequences. However, a wholesaler supplying chainsaws to a logging company can reasonably predict that their product will contribute to environmental damage and should take steps to address or mitigate this.

[1.7.1] The scope for the assessment is intentionally broad to avoid the company accidentally excluding its most material environmental impacts.

For example, an electronics manufacturer may already know that high water usage during manufacturing is a material impact. The broad scope of the assessment helps the company consider the impacts related to mining its raw materials too.

[1.7.1] The assesment’s broad scope is a starting point — it does not mean the company prioritizes all parts of its direct operations and value chain equally. The company’s focus within the scope will vary, based on the severity and likelihood of negative impacts. Companies often reassess their material impacts as they receive stakeholder feedback and learn more about their value chain. The company is not expected to have full visibility of its supply chain to assess its material impacts.

[1.7.1] The company is likely to identify potential negative environmental impacts beyond its tier one suppliers (e.g. if a tier one supplier receives raw materials from a farm in an area at water risk). In these cases, the company may prioritize the relevant tier one supplier and include a plan in its strategy [Link to ESC2.1] to increase the scope of its assessment to tier two or beyond. Increasing the scope over time enables the company to assess impacts further upstream or downstream in its value chain, to identify further potential impacts and mitigations [Link to ESC5].

[1.7.1 b; 1.7.1 c] To assess its value chain, the company may prioritize impacts in phases:

- using a high-level screening and investigating a small number of highly material issues deeply
- improving the assessment when it is reviewed and updated, every 36 months at minimum.

[1.7.1 b] The five drivers of nature change are the following.

- Land- and sea-use change
- Climate change
- Pollution (air, water, soil, plastic, and noise pollution)
- Resource use and overexploitation
- Invasive alien species

[1.7.1 b] The company follows the steps below (or equivalent) to identify its nature-related impacts. (The first two phases are adapted from the Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures — LEAP Approach [EN].)

- Phase 1: Locate — Determine the scope of the assessment of environmental impacts by locating where the company interacts with nature.

To do this, map the company’s operations and value chain, including its downstream products or services, to the ecosystem they operate in.

Determine whether those locations are ecologically sensitive.

- Phase 2: Evaluate — Identify and list all of the company’s potential environmental impacts. This helps understand how the company’s operations and value chain (up- and downstream) may interact with ecosystems, what natural resources are used, and how they are exploited.

The evaluation may be based on:

  - available environmental performance data
  - internal and external research
  - sector or industry tools to identify sector risk or high-risk materials (e.g. raw materials commonly linked to deforestation)
  - stakeholder engagement with suppliers, local communities, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), or government agencies.
- Phase 3: Assess — Prioritize impacts by assessing their severity and likelihood.

[1.7.1 b] For actual negative impacts, materiality is based on the severity of the impact, while for potential negative impacts it is based on the severity and likelihood of the impact.

[1.7.1 b] Where applicable, the company may use the metrics under ESC1.1–ESC1.6, among others, to help quantify the impacts of its own facilities.

[1.7.1 b; 1.7.1 c] To assess its value chain, the company may prioritize impacts in two phases:

- using a high level screening and investigating a small number of highly material issues deeply

[1.7.1 c] The company treats animal welfare as a material impact if it has no information on the animal welfare conditions in its operations and value chain (the five freedoms of animal welfare). Animal welfare may also be impacted if animals are used in the production or harvesting of raw materials in the company’s supply chain.

[1.7.1 e] The company prioritizes environmental impacts based on severity and likelihood. Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how widespread is the impact? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe.

An impact has a low ease of remediation when it is very difficult to restore the environment to its prior state. Note, this does not mean less severe environmental impacts should not be remedied.

- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

[1.7.1 e] The scale of a negative impact may depend on whether it violates regulations or authoritative intergovernmental instruments. For example, a negative impact can be considered greater if it risks noncompliance with the reductions in greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in line with the 1.5°C goal of the Paris Agreement.

[1.7.1 e] The scale of a negative impact often depends on its context. For example, if the company withdraws water from an area affected by water risk, the scale of the impact is greater than if it takes from an area with abundant water resources.

[1.7.2 b] The company's "prioritized value chain activities and operations" are those that it identifies as contributing the most material negative impacts. These may be, for example:

- specific operations (e.g. factories, offices)
- suppliers
- customers
- projects the company is undertaking.

[1.7.2 d] The company may use tools listed under Implementation Resources (or other external sources) for identifying industry-specific information on which environmental impacts are material.

[1.7.2 d] Climate change is a material impact for all companies. It is addressed in more detail under the Climate Action topic.

[1.7.5] FR3.1 only applies to the company if it is considered small or above. If a company is considered micro or a company without workers, then it is exempt from FR3.1 and this compliance criterion does not apply.

[1.7.5] "Considers" means to understand how the potential impact relates to the company and decide whether it is prioritized for further action.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The assessment covers the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate assessment, it demonstrates:
  - how the assessment considered its stakeholders and operational impacts
  - how the assessment outcome is relevant to its context.
- References to the highest governing body or executive team mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that a written explanation by an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional approval at the highest governing body or executive team within its scope of certification.
