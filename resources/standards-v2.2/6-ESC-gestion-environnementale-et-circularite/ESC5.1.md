# ESC5.1 (GEC 5.1) : The company considers actual and potential environmental impacts in its procurement decisions.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise travaille avec ses prestataires de biens et de services pour atteindre ses objectifs environnementaux*

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 1100-1107. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**ESC5** : The company works with suppliers to achieve its environmental objectives.

**ESC5.1** : The company considers actual and potential environmental impacts in its procurement decisions.

## Compliance criteria

### 5.1.1

The company has considered the actual and potential environmental impacts for the three most material procurement decisions:

- a) in the fiscal year or twelve months before Year 0
- b) in each subsequent year.

## Intent

To ensure the company makes better procurement decisions in relation to environmental impacts.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[5.1.1] Actions the company takes to meet sub-requirements HR4.2 and HR4.3 — to consider actual and potential human rights impacts in procurement decisions — may count towards this sub-requirement. This means the company can use one combined process to consider both social and environmental impacts. However, to meet this sub-requirement, one of the five most material procurement decisions, at minimum shows how the company has considered environmental impacts.

[5.1.1] This sub-requirement applies to suppliers at all tiers.

[5.1.1] This sub-requirement covers all types of procurement, including of:

- raw materials
- finished and semi-finished goods
- services.

[5.1.1] The company determines its “most material” procurement decisions using any of the following categories.

- Spend
- Volume
- Importance of a potential negative environmental impact

[5.1.1] The potential negative impact may be related to animal welfare, as well.

[5.1.1] To assess the importance (or materiality) of a potential negative environmental impact, the company considers its likelihood and severity.

Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how widespread is the impact? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe.

An impact has a low ease of remediation when it is very difficult to restore the environment to its prior state. Note, this does not mean less severe environmental impacts should not be remedied.

- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The action takes place within the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate group action, it demonstrates:
  - roll out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms
  - how the outcomes impact its operations. For any outcomes that are not impactful, the company creates its own actions.
