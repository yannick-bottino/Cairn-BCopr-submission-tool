# ESC2.7 (GEC 2.7) : The company assesses the potential negative environmental impacts of investments, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise évalue les impacts environnementaux négatifs potentiels des investissements, et prend les mesures d'atténuation nécessaires*

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 1001-1006. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**ESC2** : The company has a strategy to address its actual and potential negative environmental impacts, and to support staying within ecological

**ESC2.7** : The company assesses the potential negative environmental impacts of investments, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

## Compliance criteria

### 2.7.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company has a process in place to assess the potential negative environmental impacts of potential investments.

### 2.7.2

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The process sets out mitigation actions.

### 2.7.3

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company records how it assessed the potential negative environmental impacts of its three most material potential investments, and the outcomes of the process:

- a) in the fiscal year before Year 0
- b) annually in each subsequent year.

### 2.7.4

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

For Years 3 and 5, the company:

- a) records the mitigation action it took, if its assessments identified any potential or actual negative impacts
- b) records the effectiveness of its mitigation actions

## Intent

To ensure the company selects investment with environmental impacts in mind.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.7.1] If the company invests multiple times in one investment, it does not need to reassess that investment. The company may also assess related investments in groups — such as subsidiaries of the same corporate family, or bodies within the same government.

[2.7.3] The company determines its “most material” investment using any of the following categories.

- Value of the investment
- Importance of a potential negative environmental impact

[2.7.3] To assess the importance (or “materiality”) of a potential negative environmental impact, the company considers its likelihood and severity.

Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how widespread is the impact? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe.

An impact has a low ease of remediation when it is very difficult to restore the environment to its prior state. Note, this does not mean less severe environmental impacts should not be remedied.

- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The process is embedded, at minimum, at the highest level within the company. If the company references a procedure belonging to an out-of-scope parent, it records evidence of roll-out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms to support the procedure.
