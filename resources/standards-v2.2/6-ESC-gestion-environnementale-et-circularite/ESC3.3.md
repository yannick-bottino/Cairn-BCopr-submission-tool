# ESC3.3 (GEC 3.3) : The company’s product development incorporates circularity principles.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : Le développement des produits de l'entreprise intègre les principes de circularité*

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 1019-1026. Échéance : Year 3.

## Requirement

**ESC3** : The company implements circularity principles to reduce waste and its impact on natural resources.

**ESC3.3** : The company’s product development incorporates circularity principles.

## Compliance criteria

### 3.3.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 3 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company chooses one or more of the following four options.

- a) ESC3.3a The company avoids and reduces single-use products and packaging in its portfolio
- b) ESC3.3b The company designs its products for long-term use.
- c) GAC3.3c The company’s products are able to recirculate after use.
- d) ESC3.3d The company’s products are able to recirculate at their end-of-life.

### 3.3.2

*Échéance du critère : Year 3 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company can explain how its product design follows the priority order established in the waste hierarchy.

### 3.3.3

*Échéance du critère : Year 5 (marquage PDF : « For Year 5 »)*

For Year 5 the company either or both:

- a) increases the percentage of the indicators used in the sub-requirement option
- b) adds an option that is higher in the hierarchy of options.

### 3.3.4

*Échéance du critère : Year 3 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

If the company chose ESC3.3d previously, for Year 5 it chooses one of:

- a) ESC3.3a The company avoids and reduces single-use products and packaging in its portfolio.
- b) ESC3.3b The company designs its products for long-term use.
- c) ESC3.3c The company’s products are able to recirculate after use.

### 3.3.5

*Échéance du critère : Year 3 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

If the company has multiple products in its portfolio, it measures its entire portfolio along the four options in ESC3.3.1.

## Intent

For the company to reduce waste and minimize pressure on natural resources — and enhance recovery through the best available recovery cycle — by implementing circularity principles in its product development.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[3.3.1; 3.3.2; 3.3.3; 3.3.4; 3.3.5] This sub-requirement applies to products the company produces or has produced on its behalf. Products the company resells may be exempted (e.g. in the case of wholesale/retail). For companies in the Service with Significant Footprint and Agriculture/Growers sectors, this sub-requirement only applies if the company sells a physical product and has control over their production.

[3.3.1; 3.3.2; 3.3.3; 3.3.4; 3.3.5] If the product is a fast moving consumer good, this sub-requirement applies to its packaging. Fast moving consumer goods include food products, beverages, toiletries, cleaning supplies, medicines, and other products.

[3.3.1; 3.3.2; 3.3.3; 3.3.4; 3.3.5] Circularity principles do not replace other environmental factors (e.g. water use, greenhouse gasses, etc.) in product development, but are an additional consideration. Incorporating circularity requires the company to carefully consider its product and packaging choices, preferring options that help reduce the pressure on natural resources. This means that the company also follows the waste hierarchy — meaning, it avoids unnecessary packaging, or moves to reusable packaging, before considering recyclability.

[3.3.1; 3.3.2; 3.3.3; 3.3.4; 3.3.5] If legal barriers prevent the company from adopting circularity principles in its product, or in its primary or secondary packaging, the company is only required to meet this sub-requirement to the extent legally permissible.

[3.3.1; 3.3.2] When considering circular design principles, the company examines the recycling infrastructure in locations where its products are sold. If recycling infrastructure is scarce, the company may instead focus on durability and material choice.

[3.3.3] The hierarchy of options (in terms of preference from highest to lowest):

- ESC3.3a The company avoids and reduces single-use products and packaging in its portfolio.
- ESC3.3b The company designs its products for long-term use.
- ESC3.3c The company’s products are able to recirculate after use.
- ESC3.3d The company’s products are able to recirculate at their end-of-life.

[3.3.5] If the company has multiple products in its portfolio, it applies this sub-requirement to its entire portfolio of products and their packaging.

This means the company:

- evaluates which product fits under which of the four options in ESC3.3.1
- calculates the required indicators based on its entire portfolio.

[3.3.5] The company demonstrates improvement in line with 3.3.3 and 3.3.4. However, if one or more of the company’s products and their packaging do not yet fit into any of the four options in ESC3.3.1, the company may reselect option ESC3.3d for Year 5.

[3.3.5] If some of the company’s portfolio does not yet fit into any of the four options in ESC3.3.1, the company:

- provides an overview of the percentage, by weight, of products and packaging separately that do not fit any of the four options
- decreases this percentage for Year 5.
