# ESC2.2 (GEC 2.2) : The company has a biodiversity transition plan to halt and reverse biodiversity loss caused by its operations and value chain.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise dispose d'un plan de transition pour la biodiversité visant à arrêter et à inverser la perte de biodiversité*

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 960-969. Échéance : Year 3.

## Requirement

**ESC2** : The company has a strategy to address its actual and potential negative environmental impacts, and to support staying within ecological

**ESC2.2** : The company has a biodiversity transition plan to halt and reverse biodiversity loss caused by its operations and value chain.

## Compliance criteria

### 2.2.1

If the company identified biodiversity-related impacts as material in its assessment of environmental impacts, then the company’s biodiversity transition plan includes:

- a) a commitment to contribute to the global goal of halting and reversing biodiversity loss by 2030
- b) quantified targets for its own facilities
- c) a plan to engage its material suppliers (by impact) to understand:
  - i) whether they are located in or near areas important for biodiversity.
  - ii) what actions they take to manage their biodiversity impacts
- d) how the company integrates Indigenous Peoples’ local knowledge or traditional ecological knowledge and nature-based solutions.

### 2.2.2

The plan includes actions to:

- a) support aspects such as:
  - i) maintenance and enhancement of biodiversity
  - ii) carbon storage or sequestration
  - iii) soil health
  - iv) the health of other ecosystems
- b) measure positive conservation and regenerative outcomes, such as:
  - i) healthy and stable soils
  - ii) improved local biodiversity
  - iii) improved air and water quality
- c) prevent potential negative impacts associated with the company’s facilities located in or near ecologically sensitive areas.

### 2.2.3

The plan is approved by the highest governing body or executive team.

### 2.2.4

The plan follows the mitigation hierarchy for biodiversity.

### 2.2.5

The plan was adopted or reviewed in the last twelve months.

## Intent

For companies to contribute to the global goal of halting and reversing biodiversity loss by 2030, in line with the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, 2022 (Convention on Biological Diversity) [AR] [EN] [ES] [FR] [RU] [ZH]. Investing in nature’s protection and restoration is critical to this effort. This includes halting the loss of priority ecosystems, protecting land and seas, and mitigating negative impacts by, for example, reducing pollutants and eliminating plastic waste.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.2.1, 2.2.2; 2.2.3; 2.2.4; 2.2.5] This sub-requirement only applies if the company identifies biodiversity-related impacts as material in its assessment of environmental impacts [link to ESC1.7].

[2.2.1; 2.2.2] The biodiversity transition plan addresses the priority operations, high impact commodities, and value chain activities identified as material in ESC1.7.

[2.2.1 b; 2.2.2 c] The company’s biodiversity transition plan is expected to set quantified targets, as signaled under ESC2.1. Before Year 3, the company does both of the following.

- Sets targets for its own facilities.
- Has an action plan to engage its value chain to gather data and measure progress over time. The company may prioritize its material suppliers (by impact) and other material members of its value chain (by impact). These foundational steps enable the company to set concrete targets for its value chain in the future.

[2.2.1 c] The company's "material suppliers" are those it identifies as having the most actual and potential negative environmental impacts.

[2.2.1 d] Whenever possible, the company co-creates with Indigenous Peoples, incorporating their local or traditional knowledge into the plan, and building on their good stewardship and nature-based solutions.

[2.2.1 d] The company compensates Indigenous Peoples appropriately when integrating their views or knowledge, and acknowledges their time and efforts.

[2.2.2 a.iv] The health of “other ecosystems”, besides terrestrial ecosystems, refers to:

- freshwater bodies
- marine and coastal ecosystems (including oceans).
- Sets targets for its own facilities.

[2.2.4] Following the “mitigation hierarchy for biodiversity” means the company’s plan prioritizes avoidance, mitigation, restoration, or rehabili-tation of biodiversity onsite, and only uses offsetting and compensation as a last resort.

[2.2.4] Under the mitigation hierarchy, the company prefers restoration over offsetting and compensation. However, if restoration cannot be done onsite (in the area directly affected by the company’s operations), the company may compensate through restoration of an equivalent ecosystem offsite as part of its plan. The compensation should also aim for equivalent ecosystem types and ecological values.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The biodiversity transition plan is embedded, at minimum, at the highest level within the company. If the company references a biodiversity transition plan belonging to an out-of-scope parent, it records evidence of roll-out, enforcement and accountability mechanisms to support the biodiversity transition plan.
- References to the highest governing body or executive team mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that approval by an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional approval at the highest governing body or executive team within its scope of certification.
