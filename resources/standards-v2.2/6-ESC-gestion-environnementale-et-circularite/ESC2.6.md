# ESC2.6 (GEC 2.6) : The company assesses the potential negative environmental impacts related to potential organizational clients and projects, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise évalue les impacts environnementaux négatifs potentiels liés à ses prospects organisationnels et aux projets envisagés, et prend les mesures d'atténuation nécessaires*

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 985-1000. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**ESC2** : The company has a strategy to address its actual and potential negative environmental impacts, and to support staying within ecological

**ESC2.6** : The company assesses the potential negative environmental impacts related to potential organizational clients and projects, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

## Compliance criteria

### 2.6.1

The company has a process in place to assess the potential negative environmental impacts of working with potential organizational clients and projects.

### 2.6.2

The process sets out mitigation actions.

### 2.6.3

The company records how it assessed the potential negative environmental impacts of its three most material potential clients, and the outcomes of the process:

- a) in the fiscal year before Year 0
- b) annually in each subsequent year.

### 2.6.4

For Years 3 and 5 the company:

- a) records the mitigation action it took, if its assessments identified any potential or actual negative impacts
- b) records the effectiveness of its mitigation actions

## Intent

To ensure the company selects clients and projects with environmental impacts in mind.  Companies in the service sector with a minor footprint may have indirect environmental impacts through their choice of clients and projects (e.g. an advertising firm may contribute to potential impacts by marketing products that encourage overconsumption and exacerbate climate change). This also reduces the likelihood of contributing to greenwashing or other negative consequences. The intent is not to prohibit specific clients or projects.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.6.1] “Organizational client” means clients that are organizations, including businesses, public sector organizations and civil society organizations. It excludes individuals.

[2.6.1] If the company has multiple projects with one client, it does not need to reassess that client. The company may also assess related clients in groups — such as subsidiaries of the same corporate family. However, it still needs to assess each project individually.

[2.6.3] The company determines its “most material” clients or projects using any of the following categories.

- Revenue from the project or client
- Number of hours involved
- Importance of a potential negative environmental impact

[2.6.3] To assess the importance (or “materiality”) of a potential negative environmental impact, the company considers its likelihood and severity.

Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how widespread is the impact? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe.

An impact has a low ease of remediation when it is very difficult to restore the environment to its prior state. Note, this does not mean less severe environmental impacts should not be remedied.

- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The process is embedded, at minimum, at the highest level within the company. If the company references a procedure belonging to an out-of-scope parent, it records evidence of roll-out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms to support the procedure.
