# ESC3.3b (GEC 3.3b) : The company designs its products for long-term use.

Impact Area : Environmental Stewardship & Circularity (Gestion environnementale et circularité). Pages PDF 1035-1041. Échéance : Year 3.

## Requirement

**ESC3** : The company implements circularity principles to reduce waste and its impact on natural resources.

**ESC3.3b** : The company designs its products for long-term use.

## Compliance criteria

### 3.3b.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 3 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company records:

- a) the amount of its products, as a percentage of the total weight of its products (excluding packaging) that were designed for:
  - i) durability during use
  - ii) repairability during use
  - iii) reusability.
- b) the amount of its packaging, as a percentage of the total weight of its packaging, that was designed for:
  - i) durability during use
  - ii) repairability during use
  - iii) reusability.

## Intent

For the company to circulate its products and materials at their highest value, by designing for durability, repairability, and re-use.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[3.3b.1] This sub-requirement applies to products the company produces or has produced on its behalf. Products the company resells may be exempted (e.g. in the case of wholesale/retail). For companies in the Service with Significant Footprint and Agriculture/Growers sectors, this sub-requirement only applies if the company sells a physical product and has control over their production.

[3.3b.1] If the product is a fast moving consumer good, this sub-requirement applies to its packaging. Fast moving consumer goods include food products, beverages, toiletries, cleaning supplies, medicines, and other products.

[3.3b.1] If a product or packaging meets multiple criteria (e.g. it is designed for both durability and repairability), the company counts it only once in its calculations.

[3.3b.1] If the company has multiple product lines, it calculates the percentage indicators across its entire portfolio to establish the baseline.

[3.3b.1a.i; 3.3b1b.i] If the company designs its products for durability, it has information on any testing it has conducted (in-house or with external bodies). The testing confirms that the product is designed for maintenance, longevity and durability in such a way that encourages longer use than what is typical for the type of product. For products that are inherently long-lasting (e.g. timeless design with durable material choices), the company may prioritize other principles and explain why.

[3.3b.1a.i; 3.3b1bi] If the company designs its products for reusability, it has information on how their designs enable reuse. The company may evaluate “reusability” using the following criteria (adapted from Cradle to Cradle Certified® Product Standard, Version 4.1 [EN] and Glossary - Ellen MacArthur Foundation [EN]).

- The product has at least one design feature that enables it to be used for its original purpose more than once. This may require users or the company to make small adjustments or clean the component or product, depending on the type of product.
- The packaging is part of a refill system (e.g. refill pouches), or the company has a product-specific take-back program for users to return used packaging packaging, or both.

[3.3b.1a.ii; 3.3b1bii] If the company designs its products for repairability during use, it has information on how their designs enable repair. The company may evaluate “repairability during use” using the following criteria (adapted from Glossary - Ellen MacArthur Foundation [EN]).

- The product has at least one design feature that enables it to be repaired using existing systems that operate in practice and at scale (e.g. a network of repair shops or the company's own repair service) or enables users to repair the product themselves.
- For example, repairable products may:
  - have a modular design
  - use built-in predictive maintenance sensors and repair diagnostics
  - be designed with the right to repair.
  - designed for remanufacturing
  - designed using standardised components across a sector.
  - include repair manuals
  - make spare parts or software available at reasonable prices.
- For example, repairable products may:
  - have a modular design
  - be designed with the right to repair.
  - designed for remanufacturing
  - include repair manuals

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The measurement covers the company. If the company references an out-of-scope measurement, it records:
  - the data and mechanism for measuring its own operations
  - how data is consolidated in the out-of-scope parent’s reporting.
