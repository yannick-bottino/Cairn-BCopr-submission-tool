# PSG2.3 (MGPP 2.3) : The company conducts regular materiality assessment to assess and identify material topics.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise procède régulièrement à une analyse de matérialité*

Impact Area : Purpose & Stakeholder Governance (Mission et gouvernance des parties prenantes). Pages PDF 183-192. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**PSG2** : The company considers its impacts on stakeholders in decision-making.

**PSG2.3** : The company conducts regular materiality assessment to assess and identify material topics.

## Compliance criteria

### 2.3.1

The company engages the following stakeholders or their representatives in a materiality assessment.

- a) Workers of the company or its subsidiaries
- b) Suppliers
- c) Customers
- d) Investors
- e) The community where offices or facilities of the company, its subsidiaries, its suppliers, or its investments are located
- f) The local or global environment

### 2.3.2

The materiality assessment:

- a) covers the salient human rights issues and material environmental issues addressed in the B Lab Standard
- b) covers topics relevant to the company and its stakeholders, but not addressed in the B Lab Standard
- c) uses a double materiality or impact materiality approach. It cannot only assess financial materiality.

### 2.3.3

The company assesses topics based on their significance and sets a threshold to determine its material topics.

### 2.3.4

The company conducts a full materiality assessment at least every 36 months. Between full assessments, it also undertakes one interim review of materiality with a selection of its stakeholders, to update its material topics.

### 2.3.5

The highest governing body has oversight of the materiality assessment process and reviews the results.

### 2.3.6

The final outcome and the methodology the company used in its assessment are publicly available in the annual impact report or on the company’s webpage. The outcome may take the form of a materiality matrix or a list of identified material topics, plus a summary of stakeholder engagement processes.

## Intent

- To ensure the company practices stakeholder governance when identifying its material topics. It achieves this by engaging stakeholders to understand their interests and the company’s potential impacts on them.
- To ensure the company identifies all material topics — even those that may not be directly covered by the B Lab Standards (e.g. health or nutrition).

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.3.1, 2.3.2] The following actions do not meet the sub-requirement.

- A stakeholder engagement process that fails to engage key external stakeholders or to meaningfully consider their feedback
- A materiality assessment focusing only on topics related to the company's operation and financial performance (e.g. efficiency, worker productivity, or customer satisfaction)
- A materiality assessment process that fails to identify key social or environmental issues that are material to the company’s stakeholders (for example, a financial service company not identifying the social and environmental impact of its investments)

[2.3.2] The company may meet this sub-requirement by conducting one assessment that also fulfills ESC1.7 and HR2.1. In this case, the assessment is generally carried out at a higher level to identify relevant material topics beyond the scope of environment or human rights.

[2.3.2a] If the company carries out separate assessments, the material topics it identifies under this sub-requirement are aligned with those identified under ESC1.7 and HR2.1.

[2.3.2b] To ensure a comprehensive assessment the company identifies all issues relevant for the company, which may go beyond the impact topics covered in the B Lab Standard. Any material topic that goes beyond the impact topics covered in the B Lab Standard is addressed under PSG2.4.

[2.3.3] The company assesses the significance of its impacts based on severity and likelihood. Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly-severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

[2.3.3] To determine its material topics, the company prioritizes its impacts based on their significance. The company defines a cut-off point or threshold to determine which topics are material. The company prioritizes negative impacts separately from positive impacts.

[2.3.4] As well as conducting a full materiality assessment at least every 36 months, the company demonstrates an interim or ongoing review of materiality by engaging with stakeholders to update its material topics. Examples of interim or ongoing materiality assessment include, among others:

- running annual stakeholder surveys (e.g. of suppliers, customers or employees)
- scanning social media
- reviewing trends from grievance procedures.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The assessment covers the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate assessment, it demonstrates:
  - how the assessment considered its stakeholders and operational impacts
  - how the assessment outcome is relevant to its context.
- References to the highest governing body mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that oversight by an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional oversight at the highest governing body within its scope of certification.
