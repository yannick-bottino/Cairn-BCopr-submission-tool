# PSG3.4 (MGPP 3.4) : The company tracks grievances, assigns accountability for resolving them and reports internally to the highest governing body and publicly to stakeholders.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise recense les réclamations et rend compte auprès de la plus haute instance de direction et des parties prenantes*

Impact Area : Purpose & Stakeholder Governance (Mission et gouvernance des parties prenantes). Pages PDF 222-228. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**PSG3** : The company has adequate procedures to address stakeholder grievances.

**PSG3.4** : The company tracks grievances, assigns accountability for resolving them and reports internally to the highest governing body and publicly to stakeholders.

## Compliance criteria

### 3.4.1

The company conducts an annual analysis of grievances and creates an internal report. The report:

- a) has details on grievances raised and accepted
- b) evaluates the effectiveness of the company’s procedures
- c) identifies trends and hotspots
- d) recommends improvements to address these areas.

### 3.4.2

The company has a management or executive role responsible for overseeing the grievance procedure. The manager presents the annual internal summary report on the grievance procedure to the highest governing body.

### 3.4.3

The company demonstrates the effectiveness of the grievance procedure by either:

- a) recording evidence that a grievance has been resolved and closed
- b) demonstrating that they have the appropriate grievance procedure in place (inclusive of tracking), where a company has had no grievances filed.

### 3.4.4

The company publicly reports on grievance decision outcomes in accordance with the following data from the UNGP Effectiveness Criteria:

- a) The number, types, and nature of grievances received
- b) The number of requests rejected and on what grounds
- c) The number of completed cases, the outcomes, any follow-up activities, and the average duration of the process
- d) Stakeholder satisfaction levels
- e) Any other data that will help the public understand the procedure’s performance

## Intent

- To ensure the company is accountable to grievances raised by stakeholders about the company’s social, environmental, or governance issues.

By enabling this, the company puts the B Corp legal requirement for stakeholder governance into practice.

- To ensure the company has a robust and transparent process for handling grievances.
- To ensure the company addresses stakeholders’ concerns where justified, and shares its responses to stakeholders transparently.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[3.4.1.a] The company’s internal report summarizing grievances includes the following details for each grievance raised and accepted:

- status (under review, active, or closed)
- grievance case reference
- the date the grievance was filed
- who raised the grievance (unless they have requested confidentiality)
- the defendant
- the location of the grievance (if it is not sensitive)
- the nature of the grievance.

[3.4.1.d] The company shows how it is addressing stakeholder concerns with recommendations to improve grievance procedures and address hotspot areas.

[3.4.2] The management role can be part of a wider role, as long as the employee is responsible for:

- overseeing the grievance procedure
- reporting to the highest governing body.

[3.4.2] The manager’s updates to the highest governing body cover:

- the public reporting of grievance outcomes
- any trending grievance subject matter or hotspot grievances where grievances are occurring in the same subject area/department/geographical local at a high frequency
- recommendations to improve grievance procedures and address hotspot areas.

[3.4.4] The public report is accessible to all stakeholders (e.g. on the company’s webpage, or in its annual report or impact report).

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- References to roles and responsibilities mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that roles and responsibilities of an out-of-scope parent body do not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional roles and responsibilities within its scope of certification.
- References to the highest governing body mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that oversight by an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional oversight at the highest governing body or executive team within its scope of certification.
- The reporting scope covers the company. If the company references a corporate group report that includes out-of-scope entities, either:
  - the performance of the company is clearly identifiable within the group report
  - the company publicly shares its own report on its webpage.
