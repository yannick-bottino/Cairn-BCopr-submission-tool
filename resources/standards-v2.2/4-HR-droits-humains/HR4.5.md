# HR4.5 (DH 4.5) : The company increases its commitments across its sourcing documents.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise renforce ses engagements au sein de ses documents d'approvisionnement*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 691-695. Échéance : Year 5.

## Requirement

**HR4** : The company works with suppliers to achieve its human rights objectives.

**HR4.5** : The company increases its commitments across its sourcing documents.

## Compliance criteria

### 4.5.1

The company gets feedback from suppliers on the commitments and responsibilities in its sourcing documents.

### 4.5.2

The company adds buyer commitments and responsibilities to sourcing documents to achieve, at minimum, balance between buyer and supplier expectations.

### 4.5.3

The commitments and responsibilities relate to human rights.

### 4.5.4

The company updates its sourcing documents to include, at minimum:

- a) a joint commitment to human rights due diligence
- b) a joint commitment to prioritize remediation for victims of human rights violations
- c) a commitment from the buyer to the principle of responsible exit.

### 4.5.5

The company records changes to the sourcing documents.

## Intent

To rebalance expectations in buyer-supplier relations. Sourcing documents, such as supplier codes of conduct, often aim to manage the buyer’s risks. This ignores the complexity of managing human rights risks in supply chains, and creates an imbalance of risk and responsibility between buyers and suppliers. This imbalance leads, for example, to:

- suppliers making unrealistic promises to fully comply with human rights standards, where even small deviations will constitute a breach
- a focus on supplier responsibilities that overlooks buyer responsibilities — especially buyers’ purchasing practices
- contracts that prioritize the buyer and supplier if something goes wrong over negatively affected stakeholders, such as workers.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[4.5.1; 4.5.2, 4.5.3; 4.5.5] “Sourcing documents” are any documents shared publicly or with suppliers that describe the company’s expectations of suppliers.

[4.5.1; 4.5.2] This sub-requirement focuses on the company’s relationship with its external suppliers. The company is the “buyer”.

[4.5.2] Comparing buyer and supplier commitments may be done within each sourcing document or across them. For example, the company may have a Supplier Code of Conduct with supplier commitments and a Buyer Code of Conduct with buyer commitments.

[4.5.4 a; 4.5.4 b] A “joint commitment” means the buyer and supplier both agree to the commitment.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The action takes place within the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate group action, it demonstrates:
  - roll out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms
  - how the outcomes impact its operations. For any outcomes that are not impactful, the company creates its own actions.
