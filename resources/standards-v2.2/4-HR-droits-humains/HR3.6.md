# HR3.6 (DH 3.6) : The company implements additional due diligence for any operations in conflict-affected situations.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise met en œuvre une diligence raisonnable renforcée pour activités en zones de conflit*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 656-661. Échéance : Year 3.

## Requirement

**HR3** : The company prevents, mitigates, and remediates actual and potential negative human rights impacts.

**HR3.6** : The company implements additional due diligence for any operations in conflict-affected situations.

## Compliance criteria

### 3.6.1

The company identifies if it has any operations in conflict-affected situations.

### 3.6.2

If it does, the company:

- a) assesses its connection to the conflict-affected situation
- b) explains publicly its connection to the conflict-affected situation.

### 3.6.3

If the company’s assessment finds that it may be negatively impacting the conflict-affected situation, it also:

- a) engages expert organizations or representatives of affected stakeholders to learn more about their connection to the conflict
- b) assesses whether stakeholders in the conflict-affected situation can raise grievances with the company
- c) identifies triggers for actions it will take if the conflict escalates
- d) assigns a contact person within the company
- e) identifies existing human rights due diligence gaps
- f) identifies how to prevent or mitigate potential negative impacts.

## Intent

For the company to apply additional due diligence focused on conflict-affected situations. In doing so, the company acknowledges that:

- it may have an impact on the conflict, and therefore a negative impact on people affected by the conflict.
- higher-risk situations require more intensive due diligence actions

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[3.6.1] This sub-requirement does not apply if all entities within the scope of certification are based in a conflict-affect situation. For example, it would not apply to a corporate family whose company entities and subsidiaries were all located in a conflict-affected situation or situations. The intent is to require international companies that have a choice of where they operate to minimize harm by applying additional due diligence.

[3.6.1] The company checks if it has operations in any of the countries and territories listed under “conflict” in the most recently available World Bank Group’s List of Fragile and Conflict-affected Situations (the FCS List) [EN].

[3.6.1] “Operations” in this sub-requirement, refers to the company:

- having a physical presence in the conflict-affected situation (including shops, manufacturing sites, and offices)
- carrying out services with stakeholders in the conflict-affected situation (such as interviewing conflict victims)
- having investments in the conflict-affected situation, if the company is in the Equity Investors or Investment Advising industries.

[3.6.1] “Operations” in this sub-requirement does not include sourcing or procuring, or selling through third parties (e.g. retailers).

[3.6.1] A “conflict-affected situation” (also known as a “conflict-affected context”, or “conflict-affected and high-risk areas”) is a geographic area, region, or country experiencing armed conflict or widespread violence, including (from United Nations Development Program) [EN] [ES] [FR] [JP] [UK] [RU]:

- inter-state or civil war
- armed insurrections
- violent extremism
- other forms of organized violence.

[3.6.2 b] Explaining the company’s connection to any conflict-affected situations requires the company to  share:

- the nature of its operations in the specific country or territory
- how those operations may or may not impact the conflict.

[3.6.2 b] The company is exempt from making its explanation public if doing so would put people at risk of harm.

[3.6.3 c] The company “identifies triggers for actions it will take if the conflict escalates” by agreeing in advance what it will do if the conflict worsens in certain ways. This involves identifying both escalation points in the conflict and the company’s corresponding actions, as in the following examples.

- If the conflict spreads to a specific region, the company will stop operating in that region.
- If a business partner becomes involved in the conflict, the company will end its relationship with that partner.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The actions take place within the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate group action, it records evidence of:
  - roll out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms
  - how the outcomes impact its operations. For any outcomes that are not impactful, the company creates its own actions.
