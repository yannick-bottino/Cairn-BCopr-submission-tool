# HR3.3 (DH 3.3) : The company assesses the potential negative human rights impacts related to potential organizational clients and projects, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise évalue les impacts négatifs potentiels liés aux clients organisationnels et projets*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 628-642. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**HR3** : The company prevents, mitigates, and remediates actual and potential negative human rights impacts.

**HR3.3** : The company assesses the potential negative human rights impacts related to potential organizational clients and projects, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

## Compliance criteria

### 3.3.1

The company has a process in place to assess the potential negative human rights impacts of working with potential organizational clients and projects.

### 3.3.2

The process sets out mitigation actions.

### 3.3.3

The company records how it assessed the potential negative human rights impacts of its three most material potential clients or projects each year, and the outcomes of the process.

- a) in the last fiscal year before Year 0
- b) annually in each subsequent year.

### 3.3.4

For Years 3 and 5, the company:

- a) records its mitigation action, if its assessments identified any potential or actual negative impacts
- b) records the effectiveness of its mitigation actions.

## Intent

To ensure the company selects clients and projects with human rights impacts in mind. This also reduces the likelihood of contributing to greenwashing or other negative consequences. The intent is not to prohibit specific clients or projects.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[3.3.1] “Organizational client” means clients that are organizations, including businesses, public sector organizations and civil society organizations. It excludes individuals.

[3.3.1] If the company has multiple projects with one client, it does not need to reassess that client. The company may also assess related clients in groups — such as subsidiaries of the same corporate family, or bodies within the same government. However, it still needs to assess each project individually.

[3.3.3] The company determines its “most material” clients or projects using any of the following categories.

- Revenue from the project or client
- Number of hours involved
- Relation to one or more salient human rights issues
- Severity and likelihood of a potential negative human rights impact

[3.3.3] Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how many people would be impacted? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe. For example, it is easier to remediate underpaid wages than someone contracting an incurable disease due to poor health and safety conditions at work. Note, this does not mean less severe human rights impacts should not be remedied.
- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The process is embedded, at minimum, at the highest level within the company. If the company references a procedure belonging to an out-of-scope parent, it records evidence of roll-out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms to support the procedure.
