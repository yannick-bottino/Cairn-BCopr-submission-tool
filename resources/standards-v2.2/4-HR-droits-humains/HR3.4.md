# HR3.4 (DH 3.4) : The company assesses the potential negative human rights impacts of investments, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise évalue les impacts négatifs potentiels liés aux investissements*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 643-650. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**HR3** : The company prevents, mitigates, and remediates actual and potential negative human rights impacts.

**HR3.4** : The company assesses the potential negative human rights impacts of investments, and takes necessary mitigation actions.

## Compliance criteria

### 3.4.1

The company has a process in place to assess the potential negative human rights impacts of potential investments.

### 3.4.2

The process sets out mitigation actions.

### 3.4.3

The company records how it assessed the potential negative human rights impacts of its three most material potential investments each year, and the outcomes of the process

- a) in the last fiscal year before Year 0
- b) annually in each subsequent year.

### 3.4.4

For Years 3 and 5, the company:

- a) records its mitigation action, if its assessments identified any potential or actual negative impacts
- b) records the effectiveness of its mitigation actions.

## Intent

To ensure the company selects investment with human rights impacts in mind.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[3.4.1] If the company invests multiple times in one investment, it does not need to reassess that investment. The company may also assess related investments in groups — such as subsidiaries of the same corporate family, or bodies within the same government.

[3.4.3] The company determines its “most material” investment using any of the following categories.

- Value of the investment
- Severity and likelihood of a potential negative human rights impact

[3.4.3] Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how many people would be impacted? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe. For example, it is easier to remediate underpaid wages than someone contracting an incurable disease due to poor health and safety conditions at work. Note, this does not mean less severe human rights impacts should not be remedied.
- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The process is embedded, at minimum, at the highest level within the company. If the company references a procedure belonging to an out-of-scope parent, it records evidence of roll-out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms to support the procedure.
