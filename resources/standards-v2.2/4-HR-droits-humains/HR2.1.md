# HR2.1 (DH 2.1) : The company identifies its salient human rights issues.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise identifie ses enjeux majeurs en matière de droits humains*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 575-584. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**HR2** : The company knows its salient human rights issues and has a strategy to address them.

**HR2.1** : The company identifies its salient human rights issues.

## Compliance criteria

### 2.1.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company assesses human rights issues to identify which are salient to its operations and value chain. The assessment:

- a) is based on desk research and stakeholder engagement
- b) identifies risks to people (rather than risks to the company)
- c) identifies both actual and potential negative impacts (not positive impacts)
- d) identifies specific human rights issues
- e) specifies where the issues are most salient
- f) prioritizes issues based on their severity and likelihood.

### 2.1.2

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The assessment scope covers all individuals and communities affected by the company’s operations and value chain.

### 2.1.3

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The assessment was completed or updated in the last 36 months.

### 2.1.4

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company considers any “potential impacts” from the Risk Tool (FR3.1) in its assessment.

## Intent

To help the company:

- know where to focus its resources
- manage potential negative human rights impacts proactively.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.1.1] The company may carry out its own assessment or use a third party, such as a consultancy.

[2.1.1] The company may adapt the language if it operates in a country where using the term “human rights” threatens the safety of its workers.

[2.1.1 e] To specify “where the issues are most salient”, the company outlines which parts of its operations or supply chain are most likely to be affected. Here are two examples.

- The company identifies low wages as a salient issue. It specifies whether the issue relates to its own workers or workers in its supply chain, and whether it affects specific geographic areas.
- The company identifies nutrition of consumer products as a salient issue. It specifies which types of products or ingredients pose a nutritional risk.

[2.1.1 f] The company prioritizes salient issues based on severity and likelihood. Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how many people would be impacted? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe. For example, it is easier to remediate underpaid wages than someone contracting an incurable disease due to poor health and safety conditions at work. Note, this does not mean less severe human rights impacts should not be remedied.
- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

[2.1.4] FR3.1 only applies to the company if it is considered small or above. If a company is considered micro or a company without workers, then it is exempt from FR3.1 and this compliance criterion does not apply.

[2.1.4] "Considers" means to understand how the potential impact relates to the company and decide whether it is prioritized for further action.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The assessment covers the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate assessment, it records evidence of:
  - how the assessment considered its stakeholders and operational impacts
  - how the assessment outcome is relevant to its context.
