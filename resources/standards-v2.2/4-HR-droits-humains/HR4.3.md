# HR4.3 (DH 4.3) : The company considers actual and potential human rights impacts in its procurement decisions.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise prend en compte les impacts réels et potentiels sur les droits humains dans ses décisions d'achat*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 676-682. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**HR4** : The company works with suppliers to achieve its human rights objectives.

**HR4.3** : The company considers actual and potential human rights impacts in its procurement decisions.

## Compliance criteria

### 4.3.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company has considered the actual and potential human rights impacts of the five most material procurement decisions

- a) in the last fiscal year before Year 0
- b) in each subsequent year.

## Intent

To ensure the company makes better procurement decisions in relation to human rights impacts.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[4.3.1] Actions the company takes to meet sub-requirements ESC5.1 and ESC5.2 — to consider actual and potential environmental impacts in procurement decisions — may count towards this sub-requirement. This means the company can use one combined process to consider both human rights and environmental impacts. However, to meet this sub-requirement, one of the five most material procurement decisions, at minimum shows how the company has considered human rights impacts.

[4.3.1] This sub-requirement applies to suppliers at all tiers.

[4.3.1] This sub-requirement covers all types of procurement, including of:

- raw materials
- finished and semi-finished goods
- services.

[4.3.1] The company determines its “most material” procurement decisions using any of the following categories.

- Spend
- Volume
- Relevance to one or more salient issues [link to HR2.1, which is not applicable to all companies]
- Severity and likelihood of a potential negative human rights impact

[4.3.1]  Severity should outweigh likelihood, so that highly severe impacts — even if they are unlikely — are still prioritized.

- Severity refers to one or more of the following.
  - Scale — how serious would the impact be? A bigger scale is more severe.
  - Scope — how many people would be impacted? A bigger scope is more severe.
  - Ease of remediation — how easy would it be to remediate the potential negative impact? A higher difficulty of remediation is more severe. For example, it is easier to remediate underpaid wages than someone contracting an incurable disease due to poor health and safety conditions at work. Note, this does not mean less severe human rights impacts should not be remedied.
- Likelihood considers how likely the impact is, and how frequently it might occur.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The action takes place within the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate group action, it records evidence of:
  - roll out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms
  - how the outcomes impact its operations. For any outcomes that are not impactful, the company creates its own actions.
