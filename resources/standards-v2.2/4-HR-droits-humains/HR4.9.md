# HR4.9 (DH 4.9) : The company looks for living wage gaps in its service contracts.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise identifie les écarts par rapport au salaire de subsistance dans ses contrats de services*

Impact Area : Human Rights (Droits humains). Pages PDF 712-723. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**HR4** : The company works with suppliers to achieve its human rights objectives.

**HR4.9** : The company looks for living wage gaps in its service contracts.

## Compliance criteria

### 4.9.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company assesses its service contracts to see if service suppliers pay a living wage or collectively-bargained wage.

### 4.9.2

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company records any living wage gaps.

### 4.9.3

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company assesses contracts for services where no formal qualification is required.

## Intent

To ensure the company is aware of living wage gaps in its service contracts

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[4.9.1] A “service contract” is any contract for a service provided to the company. Outsourced production is not a service.

[4.9.1] The company does not assess living wage gaps where there is:

- no free living wage estimate data
- a collective bargaining agreement.

[4.9.1] The company uses one or more of the three approaches below to assess if service suppliers pay a living wage or collectively-bargained wage. The best feasible approach will depend on the availability of living wage estimates and willingness of service providers to share information.

- Research whether the service workers might fall under a collective bargaining agreement.
- Compare the minimum wage to the living wage in the location where these jobs are contracted. A potential living wage gap exists where the minimum wage is below the living wage, and the service provider is likely to pay the minimum wage.
- Compare the rate the company pays to the service provider with the living wage in the provider’s country or region. If the rate for the service is below the living wage, the company can assume the service provider cannot pay a living wage. Use hourly rates to keep things simple and comparable.
- Ask the service provider what rate it pays workers, and compare it to the living wage in its location.

[4.9.1] Living wages are localized, meaning they differ by location. As a result, the company may need to evaluate one service in multiple locations.

[4.9.1] This sub-requirement does not restrict which living wage estimates can be used (unlike FW2.8).

[4.9.2] A “living wage gap” means that a worker is paid below a living wage. The gap is often expressed as a percentage, such as 10% below the living wage.

[4.9.3] Common services that often require a formal qualification are excluded.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The assessment covers the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate assessment, it records evidence of:
  - how the assessment considered its stakeholders and operational impacts
  - how the assessment outcome is relevant to its context.
