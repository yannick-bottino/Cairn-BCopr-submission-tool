# JEDI2.k (JEDI 2.k) : The company provides additional types of paid leave beyond legal minimums. [Within the Workplace]

Impact Area : Justice, Equity, Diversity & Inclusion (Justice, équité, diversité & inclusion). Pages PDF 502-505. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**JEDI2** : The company chooses and implements JEDI actions.

**JEDI2.k** : The company provides additional types of paid leave beyond legal minimums. [Within the Workplace]

## Compliance criteria

### 2.k.1

The company provides employees at least three of the following types of paid leave.

- a) Unlimited short-term sick leave
- b) Gender affirmation leave
- c) Personal leave
- d) Religious or cultural leave chosen by workers individually
- e) Parental leave (at least 18 weeks)
- f) Adoption leave

### 2.k.2

All leave types are fully paid.

### 2.k.3

All employees are entitled to the additional types of leave.

### 2.k.4

The company uses employee feedback to determine the number of days for each leave type.

### 2.k.5

The company records the chosen types of leave in a policy document or documents that are:

- a) shared with all employees
- b) available to read at all times.

## Intent

For the company to improve paid leave benefits for employees.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.k.1] Providing leave required by national law does not count toward this sub-requirement. The only exception is for parental leave, if the legal requirement is to provide fewer than 18 weeks. In this case, providing additional parental leave above 18 weeks counts toward this sub-requirement.

[2.k.1] The company meets this sub-requirement if it offers unlimited paid time off for all types of leave, and it provides the types of leave listed in the Compliance Criteria as examples to employees (e.g. in a policy document, employment contract, or employee handbook).

[2.k.1 a] For the purpose of this sub-requirement, “short-term sick leave” refers to an absence of five working days or less. It is distinct from long-term sick leave. The company is not expected to provide unlimited long-term sick leave.

[2.k.1 c] “Personal leave” is intended to give employees leave they can use for a variety of personal reasons.

[2.k.1 d] “Religious or cultural leave” meets this sub-requirement only if employees can choose when to take it. Leave for a religious or cultural event the company chooses does not count.

[2.k.1 e] “Parental leave” refers to leave taken to care for a new baby — commonly called “paternity leave” or “maternity leave”, or a combination of the two. The 18 week minimum is based on a recommendation by the International Labor Organization.

[2.k.4] Except for parental leave, the company may choose how many days to provide for each leave type, based on employee feedback.
