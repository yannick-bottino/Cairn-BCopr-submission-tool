# JEDI2.c (JEDI 2.c) : The company reviews and updates existing policies using JEDI principles. [Foundation]

Impact Area : Justice, Equity, Diversity & Inclusion (Justice, équité, diversité & inclusion). Pages PDF 467-471. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**JEDI2** : The company chooses and implements JEDI actions.

**JEDI2.c** : The company reviews and updates existing policies using JEDI principles. [Foundation]

## Compliance criteria

### 2.c.1

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The company reviews at least five policies that affect people using:

- a) data from JEDI1
- b) stakeholder feedback
- c) its JEDI commitment statement, if applicable (JEDI2.a)
- d) its improved in-house knowledge or capacity, if applicable (JEDI2.b).

### 2.c.2

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

The review looks at:

- a) terminology used in the policies
- b) impacts the policies have on people
- c) relationships to JEDI principles.

### 2.c.3

*Échéance du critère : Year 0 (celle de la sous-exigence)*

Following its review, the company updates the policies if needed.

## Intent

To ensure existing policies reflect the company’s new or improved knowledge of JEDI principles.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.c.1; 2.c.2] The company is not required to use the term JEDI.

[2.c.1] “Policies that affect people” are policies that have an intentional or unintentional impact on people.

[2.c.2 b] Reviewing the "impacts the policies have on people" involves assessing how policies may affect some groups differently (e.g. a policy limiting remote working may affect workers with dependents more than those without).

[2.c.2 c] “JEDI principles” refers to:

- justice
- equity
- diversity
- inclusion
- related principles, such as access, belonging, equality, or fairness.

[2.c.3] The company only updates its policies if its review finds that updates are necessary.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The action takes place within the company. If the company references an out-of-scope corporate group action, it records evidence of:
  - roll out, enforcement, and accountability mechanisms
  - how the outcomes impact its operations. For any outcomes that are not impactful, the company creates its own actions.
