# JEDI1.2 (JEDI 1.2) : The company collects data on one additional social identity for worker-related measurements.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise collecte des données sur une identité sociale supplémentaire pour les indicateurs liés aux collaborateurs et collaboratrices.*

Impact Area : Justice, Equity, Diversity & Inclusion (Justice, équité, diversité & inclusion). Pages PDF 430-436. Échéance : Year 3.

## Requirement

**JEDI1** : The company collects data to inform its JEDI actions.

**JEDI1.2** : The company collects data on one additional social identity for worker-related measurements.

## Compliance criteria

### 1.2.1

The company collects data on one additional social identity (other than gender identity) for at least five worker-related measurements.

### 1.2.2

The company uses stakeholder feedback to choose the additional social identity.

### 1.2.3

Workers provide their social identity voluntarily, and can choose to remain anonymous.

### 1.2.4

The company disaggregates data to report internally on its chosen worker-related measurements.

## Intent

To better understand how impacts and opportunities vary for different groups of people. This information helps the company choose which actions to take under JEDI2.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[1.2.1; 1.2.2] The company chooses the additional social identity to analyze based on worker feedback, and its workforce, community, and country contexts.

[1.2.1; 1.2.4] The company takes precautions if asking about certain social identities is not permitted by law in its location, or may put workers at risk of harm. The company is expected to understand its local context and anticipate how asking about social identities can put workers at risk (e.g. not asking about LGBTQIA+ identification if same-sex relationships are illegal). In situations that are less clear, the company takes alternative measures, designed and implemented using worker feedback. These may include:

- adding a ‘prefer not to say’ answer option
- allows workers to skip the question.

[1.2.1; 1.2.4] The company may avoid collecting data on certain social identities if doing so would be culturally inappropriate or illegal. One workaround may be to differentiate groups without using specific labels (e.g. by asking whether workers identify as 'from this country' or 'not from this country').

[1.2.1] The company can meet this sub-requirement by collecting data about multiple social identities. The most effective approach may differ depending on the measurement being analyzed, or depending on the company’s country. For example, the company disaggregates its workplace culture data by ethnicity, and its training opportunity data by differing abilities. This would help inform actions to make its workplace culture more ethnically inclusive, and ensure training is equally accessible to workers of all abilities.

[1.2.1] Collection of disaggregated data under other parts of the B Lab Standard count toward this sub-requirement (e.g. FW2 and FW3).

[1.2.1] The company is not expected to hold information on gender or social identity as part of employment or personnel records (see Recommendations for more information).

[1.2.3] The company takes care not to accidentally de-anonymize worker feedback. This may be a risk in cases with easily-identifiable information, or where a social identity group is particularly small (e.g. if there is only one Indigenous worker).

- allows workers to skip the question.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The measurement covers the company. If the company references an out-of-scope measurement, it:
  - records the data and mechanism for measuring its own operations
  - describes how data is consolidated in the out-of-scope parent’s reporting.
