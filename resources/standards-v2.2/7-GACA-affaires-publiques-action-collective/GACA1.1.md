# GACA1.1 (APAC 1.1) : The company has a public policy on responsible lobbying.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise dispose d'une politique publiquement accessible en matière de lobbying responsable*

Impact Area : Government Affairs & Collective Action (Affaires publiques & action collective). Pages PDF 1160-1165. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**GACA1** : The company takes a responsible and transparent approach to lobbying.

**GACA1.1** : The company has a public policy on responsible lobbying.

## Compliance criteria

### 1.1.1

If the company is not engaged in lobbying, then it meets this sub-requirement by declaring this publicly.

### 1.1.2

The company’s lobbying policy states its approach to responsible lobbying, including:

- a) a statement that the company will lobby only to contribute to a positive impact on society or the environment
- b) a statement that its lobbying approach is based on either reliable or scientific data (or both)
- c) its approach to financial and in-kind political contributions
- d) its approach to anti-corruption and bribery
- e) its approach to responsible lobbying with intermediary organizations, including controls that ensure consistency with the lobbying policy
- f) which governance body within the company will formally review and approve the policy, and how frequently.

### 1.1.3

The company’s lobbying policy states its governance, control, and risk management practices, including:

- a) which governance body or executive-level position within the company is accountable for enforcing the policy
- b) how the policy is embedded within the company, including how the company puts it into practice
- c) how the company identifies, manages, and monitors risks of non-compliance
- d) how the company evaluates compliance with the governance and control framework
- e) how stakeholders can raise concerns about the company’s business conduct and lobbying practices, including reference to the company’s grievance procedure [link to PSG3].

### 1.1.4

The company’s lobbying policy is:

- a) approved by the highest governing body
- b) published on the company’s webpage and accessible to all stakeholders.

## Intent

To ensure the company takes a transparent, consistent, and responsible approach to its involvement in government affairs. Lobbying can sometimes be at odds with companies’ sustainability strategies and the wider public interest. By publicly sharing their lobbying policies, companies commit to lobbying for the public good, and demonstrate responsible business leadership.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[1.1.2.a] The company cannot achieve or maintain B Corp certification if it directly lobbies against its purpose of contributing to a positive impact on society or the environment. The company becomes eligible again once B Lab confirms it has either:

- stopped lobbying
- changed its lobbying approach to align with its purpose.

[1.1.2.a] In its lobbying policy, on contributing to a positive impact on society or the environment, the company consults either:

- the outcome sections of the B Lab Standard
- the U.N. Sustainable Development Goals or similar frameworks.

[1.1.2.b] In its lobbying policy, on its approach to responsible lobbying, the company includes:

- Reliable data (means it is evidence-based and replicable, and uses insights from independent market research)
- Scientific data (means it is based on research by scientists which has been published in a reputable peer-reviewed journal).

[1.1.2.e] If the company works with an intermediary organization with lobbying positions that undermine the company’s lobbying policy, then its approach states it will publicly report:

- the company’s position compared to the intermediary organization’s position
- an explanation of how the company is positively influencing the intermediary organization’s position
- an explanation of the company’s “red lines” — meaning the circumstances that would cause the company to stop engaging with the intermediary organization.

[1.1.3.d] The governance and control framework is described in Compliance Criteria 1.1.3.a-1.1.3.c.

[1.1.4] The lobbying policy can be a standalone document, or integrated into another document. Either form is acceptable, as long as the policy meets GACA1.1.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- The policy is embedded, at minimum, at the highest governance level within the company. If the company references a policy belonging to an out-of-scope parent, it records evidence of roll-out, enforcement and accountability mechanisms to support the policy.
- References to the highest governing body or executive team mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that approval by an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional approval at the highest governing body or executive team within its scope of certification.
