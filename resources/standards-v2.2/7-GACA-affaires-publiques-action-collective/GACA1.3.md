# GACA1.3 (APAC 1.3) : The company publicly shares its lobbying positions and political contributions each fiscal year.

*Intitulé FR (traduction à relire) : L'entreprise rend publiques ses positions en matière de lobbying et ses contributions politiques*

Impact Area : Government Affairs & Collective Action (Affaires publiques & action collective). Pages PDF 1171-1175. Échéance : Year 3.

## Requirement

**GACA1** : The company takes a responsible and transparent approach to lobbying.

**GACA1.3** : The company publicly shares its lobbying positions and political contributions each fiscal year.

## Compliance criteria

### 1.3.1

If the company is not engaged in lobbying, then it meets this sub-requirement by publicly sharing that it is not engaged in lobbying.

### 1.3.2

The public report includes:

- a) the value and recipient of financial and in-kind political contributions
- b) a summary of the company’s material lobbying positions and the relevant recipients or intermediary organizations.

### 1.3.3

The public report is:

- a) overseen by the highest governing body or a committee of the highest governing body
- b) published on the company’s webpage and accessible to all stakeholders.

### 1.3.4

The company publicly shares its lobbying positions and political contributions:

- a) in the fiscal year before Year 3
- b) annually in each subsequent year.

## Intent

To ensure the company is transparent, consistent, and responsible for its lobbying practices. By making their lobbying positions and political contributions public — with oversight from their highest governing bodies — companies demonstrate accountability for their commitments to positive social and environmental impacts.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[1.3.2.b] The company assesses its material lobbying positions and includes all lobbying that meets any of the following criteria.

- It relates to the B Lab Standard.
- It relates to material topics in the company’s materiality assessment [link to PSG2.3].
- It involves intermediary organizations in which the company has a significant role. The company may have a “significant role” in an intermediary organization if it either:
  - holds a position in the governance body, participates in projects or committees, or provides major funding beyond membership dues
  - views its membership strategically as a way to influence an intermediary organization’s objectives related to its own activities (for further information, see GRI 2: General Disclosures [EN]).

[1.3.2.b] If the company works with an intermediary organization with lobbying positions that undermine the company’s lobbying policy, then the public report includes:

- the company’s position compared to the intermediary organization’s position
- an explanation of how the company is positively influencing the intermediary organization’s position
- an explanation of the company’s “red lines” — meaning the circumstances that would cause the company to stop engaging with the intermediary organization.

[1.3.3] The public report can be a standalone document, or integrated into another report. Either form is acceptable, as long as it meets the Compliance Criteria.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- If the company has an out-of-scope parent company with lobbying positions that undermine the company’s lobbying policy, then the public report includes:
  - the company’s position compared to the parent company’s position
  - an explanation of how the company is positively influencing the parent company’s position or an explanation of how the parent company’s position does not affect the company’s social or environmental impacts.
- The reporting scope covers the company. If the company references a corporate group report that includes out-of-scope entities, either:
  - the performance of the company is clearly identifiable within the group report
  - the company publicly shares its own report on its webpage.
- References to the highest governing body mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that oversight by an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless the company has additional oversight at the highest governing body within its scope of certification.
