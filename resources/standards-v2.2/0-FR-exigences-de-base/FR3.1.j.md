## Intent

- Potential Impact: Negative environmental impacts related to ecosystem conversion

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

- The scope is the company's own operations
- Examples of ecosystem conversion include:
  - Agriculture & agribusiness
- Example: A palm oil company clearing tropical rainforest to expand plantations.
- Impact: Direct deforestation leading to habitat loss and decreased biodiversity.
  - Mining
- Example: An open-pit copper mine established in a previously forested area.
- Impact: Large-scale land clearing, removal of vegetation, and alteration of natural landforms.
  - Construction & real estate
- Example: A developer converting coastal wetlands into residential or commercial properties.
- Impact: Permanent loss of wetlands, which serve as critical habitats and natural flood controls.
  - Forestry & timber
- Example: Clear-cutting old-growth forest to supply timber and pulp.
- Impact: Elimination of complex ecosystems that are slow or unable to regenerate.
  - Industrial manufacturing
- Example: Establishing large manufacturing zones on previously undeveloped land.
- Impact: Replacement of natural ecosystems (e.g., grasslands, forests) with impervious surfaces.
- Examples that typically do not qualify as conversion:
  - Use of previously cleared or degraded land: If a company builds facilities or grows crops on land that was converted years ago (before a certain cut-off date), and no new natural ecosystems are cleared.
  - Activities on already-developed or urban land:

Expanding operations in an industrial park or urban setting does not involve ecosystem conversion.
