# FR2.1 (EB 2.1) : The company adopts the B Corp legal requirement in its corporate structure or governing documents.

Impact Area : Foundation Requirements (Exigences de base). Pages PDF 44-48. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**FR2** : The company adopts the B Corp legal requirement and commits to supporting the B Corp community’s collective purpose.

**FR2.1** : The company adopts the B Corp legal requirement in its corporate structure or governing documents.

## Compliance criteria

### 2.1.1

The company adopts an entity type or an amendment to its governing documents to require stakeholder governance (meaning it considers stakeholders in its decision-making). The company adopts stakeholder governance in accordance with the B Corp legal requirement, and as applicable to its legal form and jurisdiction.

### 2.1.2

If the company cannot adopt a stakeholder governance legal form because of local laws, it signs the B Corp Agreement and commits to:

- a) working with B Lab to determine the appropriate legal pathway
- b) advocating for benefit corporation legislation in its jurisdiction
- c) amending its formation documents or adopting a new legal form if one is enacted through local lawmaking.

### 2.1.3

The company signs an Agreement committing to the B Corp Certification process.

## Intent

- To ensure the company adopts the B Corp legal framework where possible, or helps determine or establish an appropriate legal pathway in the jurisdictions where it is not possible.
- The B Corp legal requirement:
  - ensures that B Corps remain legally accountable to all of their stakeholders — including workers, communities, customers, suppliers, and the environment — not just their shareholders
  - allows companies to protect their mission and ensures that they continue to practice stakeholder governance even after increasing capital and changing leadership.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[2.1.1] Having a clear purpose is an important way for the company to complement its adoption of stakeholder governance. To fulfill the B Corp legal requirement, the company also needs to clarify its purpose — to create a meaningful positive impact on society or the environment, or both — and show how it is engaging and accountable to its stakeholders.

[2.1.1] If the company generates less than US$10 million in annual revenue or employs fewer than 50 full-time employees, it needs to meet the B Corp legal requirement to become a certified B Corp.

[2.1.1] If the company generates more than US$10 million in annual revenue or employs over 50 full-time employees, the following timelines apply for adopting the B Corp legal requirement.

- If a benefit corporation or social purpose corporation status is available and required by B Lab in the jurisdiction where the company’s legal form is organized, the company needs to adopt the B Lab legal requirement within two years of its initial B Corp Certification date.
- If neither benefit corporation nor social purpose corporation are available or required, or the company is not a corporation, and legal amendment language is available for its jurisdiction and entity form, the company needs to adopt the required amendment language either:
  - within one year from its initial B Corp Certification date, if the entity form is a limited company, company limited by shares, corporation, or equivalent
  - within 90 days from its initial B Corp certification date, if the entity form is a limited liability company (LLC) or another company governed by contract.

[2.1.1] If the company changes its legal form or structure, it may need to meet the below steps. Changes in legal form or structure include, but are not limited to, adopting a different legal entity type (e.g. LLC to a Corporation), acquiring a majority-owned subsidiary, or restructuring.

- Notify B Lab before the change, or immediately after if advance notice is not possible.
- Adopt the legal pathway appropriate for the new legal form (if available and applicable) and send B Lab a copy of the company’s amended or new governing documents according to timelines set forth by B Lab.

[2.1.1] In limited cases, a B Corp legal requirement for a legal form or jurisdiction may significantly change. If this change happens after the company meets the legal requirement, then in future certifications, B Lab may require the company to amend their governing documents.

[2.1.1] If the company does not meet the B Corp legal requirement conditions, B Lab may revoke the company's certification or decline to recertify it.

[2.1.2] In some jurisdictions, the law does not yet allow companies to voluntarily adopt stakeholder governance. In these places, a change in law will need to be pursued. Until the law has been amended, certified B Corps and B Corp applicants in these jurisdictions sign the Agreement for B Corp Certification in which they commit to either:

- amending their formation documents
- adopting a new legal form if one is enacted through local lawmaking.

[2.1.2] If B Lab has determined the law does not yet allow companies to voluntarily adopt stakeholder governance, and the company is a public company, the company commits to adopting the legal pathway and makes its intention to adopt the legal requirement public on its website.

[2.1.2] If adopting the legal requirement is against current law in the company’s jurisdiction, the company works with B Lab to help determine the appropriate legal pathway. Any changes to the company’s formation documents are reviewed by B Lab in advance to avoid delays in the certification process.

[2.1.3] A representative appointed by the company’s highest governing body signs an Agreement committing to the B Corp Certification process.

## Applying the criteria

*Applying the Criteria to Independently Certifying Subsidiaries*

- Company refers to the certifying company and entities within scope of the certification.
- References to the highest governing body mean those within the company’s scope of certification. This means that a commitment to adopt the B Corp legal requirement by a representative from an out-of-scope parent body does not meet the sub-requirement, unless another representative at the highest governing body or executive team of the company makes an additional commitment.
