## Intent

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

- The scope is the company's own operations
- Hazardous chemical: Any substance or mixture that poses a physical, health, or environmental hazard, as classified under the EU CLP Regulation (EC No 1272/2008).
- Types of hazards:
  - physical hazards: Flammable, explosive, corrosive to metals
  - health hazards: Carcinogenic, mutagenic, toxic to reproduction (CMRs), respiratory sensitizers
  - environmental hazards: Harmful to aquatic life, ozone-depleting.
- Hazardous chemicals can be subdivided into toxicity classes according to 'Risk Phrases' assigned to individual substances. The five toxicity classes, beginning with the most dangerous one, are:
  - carcinogenic, mutagenic and reprotoxic chemicals
  - chronic toxic chemicals
  - very toxic chemicals
  - toxic chemicals
  - chemicals classified as harmful. (Glossary, Eurostat)
- Examples of chemically-intensive industries that may deal with hazardous chemicals: Dyes/Inks, Polymers/Plastics, Chemical Industry Reagents, Biocide/Insecticide/Fungicide, Coatings & Paints, Pharmaceuticals & Healthcare, Solvents, Electronic Materials, Adhesives, Cosmetics, Flame Retardants & Explosives, Detergents & Surfactives, Other Agrochem Products, Pulp, Wood & Paper, Household Products, Aerospace, Glass/Ceramics, Construction, Automotive, Biotech, Water Tech, Food Processing, Petrochemical (RiskMetrics, 2009).
- Examples of core production processes include, but are not limited to, manufacturing, cleaning related to manufacturing, agriculture, processing, assembly, or other activities central to the creation or delivery of the company’s primary products or services.
- "Considers" means to understand how the potential impact relates to the company and decide whether it is prioritized for further action.
