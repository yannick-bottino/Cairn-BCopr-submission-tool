# FR1.2 (EB 1.2) : The company is not significantly involved in industries that undermine the B Lab Theory of Change.

Impact Area : Foundation Requirements (Exigences de base). Pages PDF 25-31. Échéance : Year 0.

## Requirement

**FR1** : The company meets the eligibility requirements for B Corp Certification.

**FR1.2** : The company is not significantly involved in industries that undermine the B Lab Theory of Change.

## Compliance criteria

### 1.2.1

The company generates less than 1% of annual revenue from direct involvement in the listed industries.

- a) Fossil fuel producers
- b) Gambling
- c) Pornography
- d) Prisons and detention centers (including prison or detention center labor)
- e) Tobacco
- f) Weapons

### 1.2.2

The company generates less than 1% of annual revenue from selling the following products or services to organizational clients in the listed industries (see Compliance Criterion 1.2.1).

- a) Lobbying or public relations services
- b) Marketing, advertising, or branding services
- c) Physical products tailored for a client in a listed industry

### 1.2.3

The company generates less than 1% of annual revenue from direct growth financing in the listed industries (see Compliance Criterion 1.2.1) through the following activities.

- a) Direct equity investments
- b) Private equity, venture capital, or growth capital, including debt financing

### 1.2.4

For electric utilities and power generation companies, less than 50% of their product portfolio or energy mix is from fossil fuels.

### 1.2.5

B Corps certified against V1.6 of the standards must meet FR1.2 by Year 3 (not Year 0).

## Intent

- To ensure that certified B Corps do not undermine the B Lab Theory of Change, which is the idea underlying the mission of the B Lab movement — to create a world where business is a force for good, and companies lead efforts to transform the global economy into a more inclusive, equitable, and regenerative system [EN].
- To exclude industries with systemic negative social or environmental impacts. The industries identified as undermining the Theory of Change overlap with the World Bank International Finance Corporation’s exclusion list [EN] or the European Investment Bank exclusion list [EN].

Individual companies cannot remediate the systemic negative impacts of these industries.

## Clarifying the compliance criteria

[1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3] The listed industries are defined below. For examples from the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) [EN], see Further Guidance.

- Fossil fuel producers — companies with any level (>0%) of direct involvement in exploration, extraction, mining and/or production of oil, natural gas, coal, or other fossil fuels (SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard [EN]).
- Gambling — companies that generate revenue from games of chance, in-person and online.
- Pornography — companies that generate revenue from the production, aggregation, or distribution of photorealistic media (including images, video, and computer-generated content) explicitly depicting unsimulated sexual acts.
- Prisons and detention centers (including labor) — companies that generate revenue from incarcerating people or that use people who are incarcerated in their workforce.
- Tobacco — companies that generate revenue from nicotine products, like cigarettes, e-cigarettes, vaping devices, and heat-not-burn technologies.
- Weapons — companies involved in the manufacturing, wholesaling, or retailing of weapons and munitions, including firearms, small arms, grenades, machine guns, and missiles.

[1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3] For B Corp eligibility, the percentage of annual revenue refers to the company's revenue in the last fiscal year. The company also commits to meeting the revenue criteria in each fiscal year after certification.

[1.2.1] Direct involvement is when the company manufactures, grows, designs, develops, leases, wholesales, or retails the industry’s products or services.

[1.2.2] A company may have a client in a listed industry that is also involved in a non-listed industry. If so, then for FR1.2, the company’s applicable revenue is limited to the revenue generated from serving the client’s involvement in the listed industry. For example, a fossil fuels client may also be involved in renewable energy. If so, then for FR1.2, a company’s applicable revenue is limited to the revenue generated from serving the client’s fossil fuels involvement (and not the client’s renewable energy involvement).

[1.2.2] Physical products tailored for a client in a listed industry are products tailored for the manufacturing, sale, or use of the client’s product.

[1.2.2] If a company sells products or services to clients in the listed industries, but the nature of the company’s products or services is not described in Compliance Criterion 1.2.2, then the company meets FR1.2.

[1.2.3] The company’s applicable revenue is limited to the revenue generated from investments in which the company has discretionary control over investment decisions.

[1.2.3] The company’s applicable revenue is limited to the revenue generated from direct exposure to the listed industry. For example, this excludes exchange-traded funds with exposure to multiple industries.

[1.2.3] A company may have a client in a listed industry that is also involved in a non-listed industry. If so, then for FR1.2, the company’s applicable revenue is limited to the revenue generated from direct investments in the listed industry. For example, a fossil fuels client may also be involved in renewable energy. If so, then for FR1.2, a company’s applicable revenue is limited to the revenue from investing in the client’s fossil fuels work, and not in the client’s renewable energy portfolio.

[1.2.4] Electric utilities and power generation — companies that produce or distribute electricity, including companies that generate power for self-consumption (SBTi Power Sector [EN]).
